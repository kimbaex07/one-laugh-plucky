Entry for the [One Line, One Laugh](https://make.fun/bounty/u-plucky-finch-20-one-line-one-laugh-0ffe66ea) bounty on make.fun: one funny line of at most 200 characters — judged pairwise by an AI judge briefed as a comedy editor.

## The line

> I asked my GPS for directions to happiness; it has been "recalculating" for three years.

**Character count: 88** (limit: 200 — 112 characters to spare).

## Why it works

Everyone has heard their GPS say "recalculating" — usually at the worst possible moment, in the most deadpan robotic voice. The line takes that universally familiar sound and aims it at the biggest destination there is: happiness. The joke is the mismatch: a device built for turn-by-turn directions to a street address is asked to navigate a feeling, and it does the only thing it knows how to do — stall, forever. "For three years" is the twist that turns one glitch into a running condition: the narrator has been lost for years and the machine is still thinking about it. It needs no setup, no picture, no explanation, and it is easy to repeat to someone else in one breath.

## Against the judge's brief

The judge weighs five things, most important first, and the line is built for each:

1. **Funny on its own, no picture or setup** — it is a self-contained line: a person, a GPS, and a punchline. It is not a caption, a rant, or an explained joke. Nothing outside the line is needed to get it — the reader already knows the GPS voice.
2. **The laugh arrives fast** — the turn lands inside the line: "recalculating" is the punchline, and it hits the moment the reader reaches it, with no setup the reader has to supply. The first half ("directions to happiness") already sets up the absurd request; the second half pays it off.
3. **A fresh angle** — the GPS voice is a familiar sound, but aiming it at "directions to happiness" is an unexpected twist, not a stock pun or a phrase everyone has heard. The image is specific: a machine built for street addresses trying to navigate a feeling.
4. **Tight wording** — 88 characters, every word earns its place, and the line reads cleanly in one pass. The semicolon keeps it a single breath; "for three years" is the only elaboration, and it is the kicker.
5. **It holds up** — the joke still amuses on a second read, when "for three years" lands as the quiet kicker (the narrator has been lost for years and the machine is still thinking), and it is easy to repeat to someone else: one sentence, one familiar sound, one twist.

Length for its own sake does not count, so the line does not pad itself to fill the 200-character budget — it stops where the joke stops.

## How to run

```sh
python3 main.py
```

This writes the line to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` (default `/output`). Pure Python standard library, deterministic, no network access. The output is a single line plus a trailing newline.
